About Abbey House Coffee

Abbey House Coffee is a unique coffee shop located inside a beautiful historic cathedral. We kept the peaceful, calming vibe of the old church to create a quiet escape from the busy world, but in a totally relaxed, welcoming way. Under the high ceilings and stained glass windows, we serve amazing, high-quality coffee meant to bring people together. It’s the perfect place to slow down, catch up with a friend, and enjoy a really good cup of coffee in a truly special setting.

Our Story

Abbey House Coffee started when Sebastian, a local barista, stepped inside the neighborhood's abandoned 19th-century cathedral just before it was scheduled for demolition. Standing under the towering stained glass, he felt an immediate, undeniable sense of peace. He realized that while the building was no longer a church, its spirit didn't have to die, it just needed a new ritual to bring people together. Inspired by ancient monasteries that served as community hubs for travelers, Sebastian decided to replace the traditional altar with a minimalist espresso bar, saving the historic structure. What makes Abbey House completely different is how it embraces the cathedral's natural, meditative hush without forcing a theme. There are no gimmicks or loud music here. Instead, the café uses the towering stone walls and soft lighting to create a calm escape from the busy modern world. It is a secular sanctuary built for both quiet reflection and warm conversation, where a portion of every "Sanctuary Blend" sold goes directly toward preserving the historic building.

Our Core Values

The pillars that define our sanctuary and your morning cup.

Secular Sanctuary

We preserve the natural, meditative hush of the cathedral. It is a peaceful escape designed for quiet reflection and soft conversations.

Mindful Sourcing

All of our coffee is ethically grown and sustainably sourced. We partner exclusively with fair-trade farmers who care for the earth.

Historic Preservation

A portion of every single cup sold goes directly toward the structural upkeep and restoration of this historic 19th-century cathedral.
